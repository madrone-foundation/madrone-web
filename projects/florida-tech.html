Florida Institute of Technology

A 555-bed residence hall on Florida Tech's Melbourne campus — the largest expansion of on-campus housing in the university's history.

Growing housing for a growing university

Florida Institute of Technology, on Florida's Space Coast in Melbourne, has seen enrollment demand outpace its on-campus housing supply. To close the gap, the university turned to a public-private partnership with Madrone Community Development Foundation serving as the project's nonprofit owner.

The result is a new six-story residential community of approximately 212,000 square feet on Babcock Street, adding 555 beds to the university's housing inventory — an increase of more than 25 percent — with modern amenities and community spaces designed for today's students.

The structure

The project was financed through educational facilities revenue bonds issued by the Capital Trust Authority, with proceeds loaned to Madrone – Florida Tech Student Housing I, LLC, a single-purpose subsidiary of Madrone Community Development Foundation. The structure preserves the university's debt capacity while delivering housing at the pace its enrollment requires, with development services provided by an experienced national student housing developer.

Highlights

  • Six-story residence hall of approximately 212,000 square feet
  • 555 new beds — a 27 percent increase in the university's housing supply
  • Financed with tax-exempt educational facilities revenue bonds
  • Nonprofit ownership through a Madrone single-purpose entity
  • Scheduled to open for the Fall 2026 academic year
